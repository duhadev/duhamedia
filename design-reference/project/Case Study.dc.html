Work/Study 01 — Northside Goods
Build + engine, 12 months · Hardgoods retail, 3 locations Published Feb 2026 · Client-approved figures · 8 min read
In short

Sessions were up 31% year over year and revenue was flat, so we spent two weeks reading the funnel instead of buying traffic. Tracking first, then the store, then acquisition. Twelve months later: +212% online revenue, and their own manager running the engine.

01

What we found

Two weeks of audit, nothing shipped. Traffic growth with flat revenue is either a conversion problem or a measurement problem. Here it was both. The theme carried four legacy apps and 2.4 seconds of blocking script before first paint, and checkout asked for five fields on a store where 68% of sessions were mobile.

The second finding mattered more. Conversion tracking double-counted returning customers, so every channel report the owner had been making decisions on overstated paid performance by roughly 40%. Nobody had lied to them. The numbers were just built badly.

02

The order of work, and why

Tracking first, because every later decision depends on it. Then the store rebuild, because paid traffic into a leaking checkout buys more leaks. Acquisition only from month four — and by then a two-point gain in checkout completion was worth more than any bid change available in month one.

Scaling spend into a store that leaks demand just buys more leaks.

The tempting alternative was to restructure the ad account in week one and post a fast ROAS win. It fails for two reasons: the win is measured on the same broken tracking that caused the problem, and the checkout ceiling doesn't move. It looks good for about six weeks.

03

What changed, month by month

Weeks 1–2, audit. Weeks 3–4, tracking rebuilt and the prior twelve months restated so before and after count the same way — reported paid performance dropped 40% on paper before anything actually improved. Months 2–3, theme rebuild: checkout cut to two fields, 900ms off first paint, the app-injected upsell removed from the payment step. Completion moved 34% to 58% inside six weeks, with no acquisition work at all.

Month 4, email lifecycle flows — share of revenue went from 4% to 21%. Months 5–8, paid scaled against the corrected baseline to 3.2× blended. Month 9, handover: their marketing manager took the calendar, the ad account, the reporting and the playbook.

04

What we'd do differently

Handover training should have started in month five, not month eight. Their manager was ready earlier than our plan assumed, and two months of shadowing would have been enough. Our schedule was the constraint, not her pace.
