Why you're searching this

The pattern behind this page is consistent: a business netting $500K–$5M offline, a Shopify store someone half-built, and no appetite for wasting another $10k. You aren't shopping for a web design agency. You want the store to convert and one name accountable when it doesn't.

Two things usually went wrong before us. The build was scoped by taste instead of by funnel, and nobody wired the numbers, so there's no way to tell whether the last spend worked.

02

Revision before rebuild

Our default is the Revision, not the scratch build. We keep what converts, replace what leaks, and only propose a full theme when the platform itself is the constraint. It is cheaper for you and it makes the before-and-after honest.

A rebuild you didn't need is the most expensive thing a developer can sell you.
03

The order of work

Positioning, then tracking, then design. SEO foundations sit inside the build — URL structure, collection architecture, redirects — because those are the decisions you cannot cheaply undo later. Tracking ships before the theme does, so month three has a baseline that agrees with Shopify.

You approve at three dated checkpoints and watch the same dashboard we do. Nothing goes live on a Friday.

How we do it

Builds from $5K, fixed after the audit, by the team that also runs the growth engine afterwards — so the store is built for the marketing that follows. And because the endgame is your team running it, the build ends with training, not a support retainer.

Hiring a freelancer vs. hiring a team

If the work is a handful of template edits and you already have someone who owns the numbers, a good freelancer at $2–4K is the right call — no fear-mongering here. The integrated case starts when the store has to carry acquisition spend, because then the build decisions and the marketing decisions are the same decisions.
