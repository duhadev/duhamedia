Why this company exists

The businesses we work with aren't startups. They net $500K–$5M a year and already know how to sell — offline. Online, the same owners had managed five different freelancers, or paid an agency that disappeared after launch.

[Placeholder — founder-authored origin. The pattern: established Chicago businesses, strong offline, under-scaled online, burned by fragmented vendors. No invented history.]

Duha is built against both failure modes: one accountable team, with an exit on the calendar.
02

How an engagement actually runs

Phase 1 builds the foundation — theme, tracking, SEO groundwork — on a fixed scope with dated checkpoints, from $5K. Phase 2 operates the engine: email, ads, content, SEO, GBP, CRM, one team on one dashboard, from $1K/mo. Phase 3 is the handover, and it is the reason the first two are ordered this way.

Launch is the start of Phase 2, not the end of the invoice. Tracking comes before acquisition, because scaling spend into a store that leaks demand just buys more leaks — and because every later decision is made on those numbers.

03

How we decide things

We don't disappear after launch. You'll never wonder who's running the store. The endgame is your team running it without us — stated as fact, scheduled as a phase; retainers that never end are a business model, just not ours. Numbers over adjectives: if a claim can't be checked, we don't make it.

We cap at seven concurrent builds. That isn't scarcity theatre — it's what keeps every build senior-staffed.
